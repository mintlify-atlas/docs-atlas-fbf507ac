When you open TimeFlow for the first time, the app guides you through a short onboarding sequence. The flow introduces key features, requests notification permission, and then lands you on the main timer screen. Onboarding only runs once — after you complete it, the app goes straight to the timer screen on every subsequent launch.

## Onboarding flow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Welcome screen">
    The app opens on the **Welcome screen** (`WelcomeScreen` route). This screen greets you and invites you to start the walkthrough.

    Tap the continue button to proceed.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Feature highlights">
    The **Feature highlights screen** (`ShowFeaturesScreen`) walks you through the main capabilities of TimeFlow:

    - Life progress bars for day, week, month, and year
    - Home screen Glance widgets
    - Calendar, tasks, and events
    - Smart notifications

    Swipe or tap through each highlight to see what the app offers.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Notification permission">
    The **Notification screen** (`NotificationScreen`) asks you to grant notification permission.

    <Note>
      Notification permission is required for task and event reminders. TimeFlow uses this permission to alert you when a task is due or an event is about to start. If you skip this step, you can grant the permission later in your device's app settings.
    </Note>

    Tap **Allow** when Android shows the system permission dialog. If you prefer not to grant it now, tap **Skip** — you can enable notifications at any time from **Settings → Apps → TimeFlow → Permissions**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Main timer screen">
    After you complete onboarding, the app saves your progress to **DataStore** and navigates to the **Timer screen** (`TimerScreen`). This is the main view you'll see on every future launch.

    The Timer screen displays your visual life bars for the current day, week, month, and year.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## How onboarding state is saved

TimeFlow uses Jetpack DataStore to persist whether you have completed onboarding. The `TaskAndEventViewModel` reads this state when the app initializes:

- **`readOnBoardingState()`** — checks DataStore to determine whether onboarding is complete.
- **`saveOnBoarding()`** — writes the completion flag to DataStore after you finish the final onboarding step.

If onboarding is already marked as complete, the app skips directly to the `TimerScreen` without showing any of the onboarding screens.

## Returning to onboarding

Once onboarding is complete, there is no built-in way to replay it from within the app. If you need to reset onboarding (for example, after clearing app data), clearing the app's storage in **Settings → Apps → TimeFlow → Storage → Clear data** will reset the DataStore flag and trigger onboarding again on the next launch.
